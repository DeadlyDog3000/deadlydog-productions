Download everything How to install

Install in a minute

For Epic, GOG, or a Steam copy without the Workshop. On Steam you can simply subscribe on the Workshop instead.

Download

Pick a mod below, or take The Complete Collection, which holds all of them in one.

Unzip into Mods

Documents\My Games\OldWorld\Mods

Make the Mods folder if it isn't there. Each mod is a folder of its own inside it.

Switch it on

Start Old World, open Mods from the main menu, tick the mod, and start or load a game. Your saves keep working.

Using The Complete Collection? Leave the single mods off, since they're already inside it.

Armies & looks
No. XV

Armies

Twenty men to a legion. Every unit an army.

What it does

Every unit on the map is drawn with far more soldiers. A legion is a dense block of 20 men, warbands are loose mobs of 20, archers stand in staggered lines of 20, cavalry ride in squadrons of 12, and chariots, elephants and siege engines come in batteries. Settlers, scouts, caravans and disciples travel in small groups (workers stay single, because the game sends every worker figure to the same build spot). Figures are scaled down so every unit still fits its hex. Warbands and skirmishers stand a little out of line so they look like people, not a spreadsheet.

Armies & looks
No. XIX

Fallen Soldiers

Wounded units lose men. The rest break ranks.

What it does

When a unit takes damage, men in its formation fall and die. A legion at half health shows half its men; a unit at a quarter of its health is down to a quarter. The men drop when the clash is over, using the game's own death animation, lie where they fell for a moment, and fade away.

The survivors lose their neat ranks as their comrades fall. A fresh unit stands in parade-ground lines; one that has lost half its men stands loose and ragged, every man a little out of place and turned a little off the line; a unit down to its last few is a scattered knot of men. When the unit heals, men return and the ranks close up again.

Loyal Families

The great houses forgive more and value their place at court.

What it does

Every grudge a family holds against you is half as strong: a slight at court, a refused law, a city given away, an exiled member. Grudges still last just as long, so a family still remembers how you treated it.

Ties of blood and office count for more. A family whose member sits on the throne, stands as heir, or married into your line warms to you more. So does a family whose cities enjoy your luxuries.

Pack

Merchant Empires

Win the markets, and the gold follows.

What it does

The world no longer has one price for food, wood, stone and iron. Each region trades at its own market city, and its prices follow what the region grows, uses and buys. Cities send merchants to market cities along roads, rivers and sea lanes, and those trade routes pay every turn. Whoever holds a market city takes duties from every foreigner who trades there, so taking a rival's market, by war or by outlasting them, is how you build a merchant empire, as Venice did.

Play it with Lost Kingdoms and its six nations get trade perks of their own.

Pack

Supply Lines

Cut the road and the army starves.

What it does

Armies need supply. Your cities, forts and garrisons feed the units around them. Supply carries about 3 tiles over open country, but about 10 tiles along your roads and rivers, and across seas your ships hold. Deep in enemy land, a road is a lifeline. An enemy unit standing on it cuts it.

A unit in supply can heal anywhere supply reaches, even in enemy land, where the base game allows healing only on friendly ground. Away from friendly ground it heals at half the pace: a Heal takes two turns to do what one does at home. A unit that is cut off cannot heal and suffers attrition every turn, and it gets worse the longer it goes without supply: Turns cut offStateEach turn 1–2Short of Supplies−1 HP, −5% strength 3–4Hungry−2 HP, −10% strength 5+Starving−3 HP, −20% strength, −1 march Back in supply, the hunger is gone at the start of the next turn. Short of Supplies and Hungry wear a unit down to its last hit point; Starving can kill: a starving unit whose hunger would take its last hit points dies.

Lost Kingdoms

Six forgotten kingdoms take the field.

What it does

Six new nations from the edges of the Old World that the base game leaves out: the merchant lagoons of Venetia, the Argead kingdom of Macedonia before Philip, the caravan kingdom of Nabataea, Lydia of the first coins, Saba of the incense and the great dam, and silver Tartessos at the end of the sea. None of them overlaps a nation already in the game, or takes another nation's cities.

Each has its own perk, two unique units, two dynasties, four families, twenty cities and its own names, and starts at home on the game's real-world maps.
